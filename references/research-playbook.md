# Research Playbook

## Candidate discovery

Search multiple independent source families before final selection. Vary queries to surface:
- best/essential books on the exact topic;
- practitioner/expert reading lists;
- editorial and newspaper lists;
- international lists and translated recommendations;
- community favorites and long-form discussions;
- books frequently criticized despite popularity;
- overlooked/underrated books;
- opposing schools of thought;
- recent books that update classic material.

For notable-person recommendations, search direct interviews, letters, shareholder communications, official reading lists, personal sites/newsletters and reputable reporting before secondary listicles.

## Candidate investigation

For each finalist, try to establish:
1. bibliographic identity and original year;
2. current meaningful edition;
3. substantive edition changes;
4. author credentials relevant to the topic;
5. contents/chapter structure;
6. positive expert/reader case;
7. typical/balanced reception;
8. strongest substantive criticism;
9. currency/date sensitivity;
10. relationship to other candidates.

## Review synthesis

Do not copy isolated reviews. Synthesize recurring themes across multiple sources when possible.

Use three lenses:
- **Strongest positive case** — what advocates consistently value.
- **Typical reader view** — common strengths plus ordinary limitations.
- **Strongest criticism** — recurring substantive weaknesses, not trolling or irrelevant complaints.

Mention when criticism is polarized or source communities disagree.

## International discovery

For broad searches, consider high-quality sources from the US, Canada, UK, Europe, Australia/New Zealand, India, China, Japan and South Korea. Expand beyond these when the topic warrants it.

Do not equate a country's newspaper list with universal quality. Use geographic breadth to reduce Anglo-American/popularity bias and surface different intellectual traditions.

## Edition checks

Prefer publisher/catalog/library/author evidence for editions. Separate:
- new edition with revised/added material;
- anniversary edition with new introduction/foreword;
- translation revision;
- paperback/hardcover/cover redesign;
- simple reprint.

Only describe changes that can be supported.
