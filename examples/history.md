# History recommendation examples

## Cross-cultural discovery

**User**

> I want ten books that help me understand the 20th century globally, not only from a US/European perspective.

**Expected behavior**

Search internationally and avoid treating one national canon as universal. Build a candidate pool spanning political, economic, social, colonial/post-colonial, Asian, African, Middle Eastern, and other relevant perspectives where the evidence supports them.

Use the 80/20 rule relative to the reader's stated goal: most books should directly build global 20th-century understanding, while two may be connected exploration picks such as memoir, economic history, intellectual history, or a regional work the reader would be unlikely to discover through mainstream English-language lists.

## Opposing interpretations

**User**

> Recommend books about the causes of World War I. I don't want ten authors making the same argument.

**Expected behavior**

Treat viewpoint diversity as part of relevance. Identify major interpretive differences and avoid ten redundant narratives. Clearly separate scholarly disagreement from factual disagreement and do not manufacture false balance merely to create variety.
